## 1. The Cold Call Playbook: One Opener, Four Pillars (cold-call-playbook.txt)

**Job of call one: you are not selling the house.**
- Tyler: "It's about getting that correct number so that you have a longer time to nurture that relationship. When first to market, you are a correct number gatherer. That's your job."
- Call one must produce 3 things: (1) confirmed decision-maker with a correct number, (2) at least 2 of the 4 pillars, (3) a booked next call (two time options). The deal is won in the follow-up.
- Master one script ("practice one kick 10,000 times"), don't deviate; internalize until you sound like a calm local human.

**The opener (verbatim):**
"Hi, my name is ______. I know this might seem a little out of the blue but I am reaching out to see if you have any interest in selling a property now or in the near future?"
- Three moves: (1) Name, then air: ~10 sec window; slow, low energy, local. Fast/polished reads as a pitch. (2) The disarm: admitting the interruption ("out of the blue") kills pushback; must survive 150 dials/day (prospector floor). (3) The soft ask: "a property", not "your house"; "near future" keeps a no from being final.
- Ask the question, then stop talking. Let silence work.

**The 4 Pillars, cold-call order: Motivation -> Timeline -> Condition -> Price.**
1. Motivation (the why; anchor pillar; emotion drives decisions). A problem with a clock (tenants, taxes, a move) = hot; "curious what it's worth" = cold.
   - "If you did decide to sell, why would you?" / "Tell me more about that." / Test: "What happens if it doesn't sell?" (exposes real motivation fastest).
2. Timeline (the when). A hard date beats a big number as a motivation signal; "ASAP"/"before the auction" outranks price talk.
   - "What timeframe do you feel is comfortable for you?" / "What would happen if you do not sell it by that time?"
   - Strongest: a deadline they did not choose (sale date, move date, court date).
3. Condition (the what). Frame as interest, never inspection; never say a negative word about the house; write down their exact words.
   - "This sounds like a property we might be interested in. It really just depends on the condition. Can you tell me about it?"
   - Big tickets: age of roof and furnace (move the offer most). Never insult the property (reads as attack).
4. Price (the how much). Always last; never give yours first.
   - "What is a ballpark figure that you would like to sell the property for?" / if high: "How did you come up with that number?"
   - Offer belongs on the next call.

**Temperature from pillar count:**
- Hot = 2+ pillars: work every 1-2 days until it closes.
- Warm = 1 pillar: follow up every 15 days; never make them repeat themselves.
- Cold = 0 pillars: no pass-off; touch every 45 days.

**Close that prevents ghosting (verbatim):**
"Based on what you've told me, this sounds like a good fit. My partner handles the final numbers. The next step is a 5-minute call where we'll review what the property qualifies for. At the end of that call, all we need is a simple yes or no. If it's not for you, that is perfectly fine. Are you available tomorrow at 10 AM or 2 PM?"

**A full attempt = every number on the record + a voicemail + a text.**
- Voicemail (verbatim): "Hey [first name], this is [your name]. I was hoping to catch you real quick. Give me a call back when you get a chance, [your number]. Thanks."
  - Name + number only. Never name the property, no price, no company pitch. An address-naming VM gets skipped, shopped to other buyers, or heard by the wrong family member.
- 4 texts:
  1. Identity check: "Is this address yours?"
  2. Drip: "Not sure my text went through."
  3. Soft ask: "Ever thought about selling?"
  4. Breakup: "Did you decide to keep it instead?" (a no you can drip on beats a maybe you chase forever)
- Every text: <160 chars, no links, no prices, never name the distress list, signed with first name.
- Standard lists: first text lands 10-20 minutes before the dial; later texts ride along with each attempt, varied every time.
- Cost: text ~$0.01; dial $0.03-0.06. Spend cheap touches freely; expensive touches wait for records that earned them.
- **NEVER cold text deceased, obituary, or sensitive probate lists.** Those run call + voicemail + direct mail, then deep prospecting to heir/executor. Never open by mentioning the death.
- **No AI cold prospecting.** Phil: "Do not use AI to prospect coldly. If you do that, you're probably gonna get sued, just a matter of time, because robocalling has always been illegal." AI voices = robocalls under TCPA.

**Disposition discipline:**
- 150 dials/day prospector floor; 1 dial = 1 phone number.
- 4 full attempts per record before the channel changes.
- Attempt counter moves BY HAND: no integration writes it. Disposition every number after every dial, then increment the attempt yourself.
- 75-100 correct-number not-interesteds ~= 1 deal in the next 12 months. Mark them, drip them.
- After attempt 4 with no contact -> mail channel. 90 days later it rehashes back into the dial queue. Nothing gets deleted.
- Correct-number policy is loose on purpose: an owner-named voicemail greeting counts as correct.

**Cadence by status:**
| Status | Owner | Rhythm |
|---|---|---|
| No Contact | Lead Manager | Daily for 3-5 days |
| Hot (2+ pillars) | Closer | Every 1-2 days until closed or ghosted |
| Warm (1 pillar) | Lead Manager | Every 15 days |
| Cold (0 pillars) | Lead Manager | Every 45 days |
| Not Interested | Marketing drip | Quarterly |
| Ghosting | Marketing drip | Quarterly |
- If cold leads sell to a competitor, cadence is too loose: tighten 45 -> 30 days; adjust in 15-day increments until you stop losing them.

**Opener mistakes (Don't -> Do):**
- "Are you interested in selling your house?" (dead, filtered in seconds) -> "any interest in selling a property now or in the near future?"
- Pitching numbers / pushing a close on call one -> book the 5-min partner call, "tomorrow at 10 AM or 2 PM?"
- Naming the distress ("I see you're in foreclosure") -> name the help: "challenges", "on your terms", "we can often close before that happens".
- Giving your price first (caps the deal, ends discovery) -> their ballpark, then "How did you come up with that number?"
- Talking through silence -> ask, then stop.
- Reading like a robot -> internalize; be you, framework underneath.

**First-minute pushback (verbatim):**
- "Why do you want to buy my house?" -> "Our marketing team puts together lists in areas we're interested in. I'm just calling to see if you've had any interest in selling now or in the near future."
- "How did you get my number?" -> "Public records. I'm a local investor buying in the area." (straight, calm, offer an easy out; defensiveness confirms suspicion)
- "No, I'm not interested." -> "Okay, no worries. Now, is that no now or just no never?" ("no never" = disposition; "no now" = future lead)
- Any other objection: treat as pillar data; answer short, ask the next question.

**Same opener, list-specific follow-through:**
- Probate/inherited: you are a resource, not a vulture; slow clock, high sensitivity; most probates say no on call one, don't push.
  - "I know this can be a confusing process. We help families with inherited properties all the time. What's your biggest question right now?"
  - Do NOT say "I'm sorry for your loss." Soften "death"/"inherited" to "estate" or "family property".
- Foreclosure/auction: you are speed and certainty; fastest clock; escalate hard inside 2 weeks of sale date.
  - "I see the auction is scheduled for [Date]. We can often close before that happens, which gives you more control. Have you considered that?"
  - Do NOT say "You're about to lose your house." Sell "on your terms" vs the bank's.
- Tax delinquent: problem solver; medium clock, long horizon (redemption periods can run years).
  - "We specialize in helping owners resolve these exact situations. Often, we can buy the property and handle the back taxes. Is that something you'd be open to exploring?"
  - Do NOT say "You owe the government money."
- Cold Call Coach skill (Claude Skills for REI) grades recorded calls on opener, pillars, tonality.

## 2. The Ultimate Call Playbook: Discovery to Commitment (ultimate-call-playbook.txt)

- Every call is one rehearsed arc: Discovery -> Deepening -> Money Conversation -> Commitment. Correct-number-gatherer on first touch; deal won in follow-up. Emotion drives the decision, logic justifies it; every stage digs for the emotional why. Scripts word for word but internalized. Ask, then silence.
- Stage headline numbers: Discovery = 4 pillars (MCTP); Deepening = 10-15 minutes, expectations set; Money = 21 days to close, worst case; Commitment = 0 open offers, ever. Money stage is where deals are won or lost.

**Stage owners/exits:**
- Stage 1 Discovery (cold caller): open soft ("any plans for the property", never a pitch); capture MCTP; exit = 5-10 min follow-up call on calendar.
- Stage 2 Deepening (lead manager): set TARP; qualify (2+ Hot pillars = ready for offer); exit = roadblocks named, offer call booked.
- Stage 3 Money (closer): handoff open, never make seller repeat story; frame motivation, timeline, then the number; exit = seller picks a direction on the call.
- Stage 4 Commitment (closer): walk the 6 most important parts of the agreement together; signature on the call or in the living room; exit = signed agreement or firm callback with time and date.
- Follow-up cadence: Hot every 1-2 days, Warm every 15, Cold every 45.

**[NOTE] This page's Stage 1 pillar order is Motivation -> Timeline -> Condition -> Price (the "MCTP" label says M-C-T-P but the listed order is M, T, C, P, matching the Cold Call page).**

**Stage 1 Discovery:**
- Opener (verbatim; different from cold-call page): "Hey, is this [name]? This is [your name]. I was just calling about the property on [street], wanted to see if you had any plans for it?"
- Pushback fallback: "Would you have any interest in selling now or in the near future?"
- Pillar questions (verbatim):
  1. Motivation: "So about the property, I am curious, if you did decide to sell whether to us or anyone else, why would you?"
  2. Timeline: "What timeframe do you feel is comfortable for you and your family?"
  3. Condition: "It really just depends on the condition of the property. Can you tell me about it, like the age of the roof, age of the furnace, things like that?"
  4. Price: "What is a ballpark figure that you would like to sell the property for?"
- Close (verbatim): "This sounds like a property we might be interested in. I would really like to give this thing our best shot and get my partner on the line to ask you questions about the condition. It should only take 5-10 minutes. At the end of that call, all we need is a simple yes or no. If it's not for you, that is perfectly fine. Are you available tomorrow at 10 AM or 2 PM?" (short timeframe, clear agenda, permission to say no = kills ghosting)

**Stage 2 Deepening (process call, 10-15 min). Open with TARP (best defense against ghosting):**
- T Time: "This call should take about 10-15 minutes, no longer."
- A Agenda: "We're going to talk about your motivation, the condition of the property, and your ideal timeline."
- R Result: "By the end of this call, we'll have a much clearer picture of whether we can help."
- P Permission: "If this isn't the right fit, that's perfectly fine. You won't hurt my feelings."
- 4 question banks, in order:
  - Problem: "If you did decide to sell, why would you? What made you consider selling this property now?" / "Tell me more about that. What's the biggest challenge you're facing with the property right now?" / "How long have you been dealing with that situation?" (no problem = no urgency = no deal)
  - Picture-perfect: "Let's imagine everything goes perfectly with selling this property. What does that look like for you?" / "Where would you go after selling this property? What would that allow you to do?"
  - Impact: "How does that make you feel?" / "Is that a big deal or is that a little deal for you?" / "When you say [mirror their last few words], what do you mean by that?"
  - Roadblock: "Would anyone else be upset if you made a decision about this property without speaking with them first?" / "Let's assume you like our offer and you're happy with the terms. What's the next thing you would have to figure out to make this happen?" / "We are flexible with our closing timeline. Typically, we can close in 21 to 24 days. What timeframe do you feel is comfortable for you and your family?" (every roadblock named now is an objection you never hear later)
- Close the process call (verbatim): "Thank you for sharing all that with me. Based on everything you've told me, I'm going to put together some options for you. Our next call will be brief, about 5 minutes. The result of that conversation is that we want you to make a simple yes or no decision. If you decide you don't want to move forward with us, that's perfectly fine. How does [specific date and time] work?"
- Gate to Stage 3: 2+ Hot pillars.

**Stage 3 Money:**
- Handoff open (verbatim): "Hi [seller name], this is [your name]. I've been reviewing the notes from your conversation with [team member] and I understand you're looking to sell because [their motivation]. You also mentioned you'd ideally like to close by [their timeline]. Is that all still correct?"
- Frame before you price: replay their problem in their words; number lands as the answer to their story.
- Timeline expectation (verbatim): "We're going to put 21 days just as a worst-case scenario. I always want to tell you 21 days and then it gets done in seven or ten days, versus telling you seven or ten days and it actually taking 21. I just want to make sure I'm setting the right expectations."
- Price-vs-terms (verbatim): "Which is more important to you right now: getting the absolute highest price, even if it takes a bit longer to receive, or getting cash in your hand as quickly as possible?"
- Play hard to get, never desperate; you have funds and a process.
- Offer structure (cash/novation/listing) lives in Making the Offer.

**Stage 4 Commitment:**
- Never send an agreement into the void (a seller alone with your offer shops it).
- E-signature close (verbatim): "Great. I'm sending the agreement to your email now. Let's pull it up together and I'll point out the six most important parts to make sure you're 100% comfortable. We never leave open offers, so we'll get this signed together on the call."
- 6 parts, in order: (1) purchase price and terms, (2) closing date, (3) as-is clause, (4) who pays closing costs, (5) contingencies, if any, (6) earnest money deposit.
- Seller on phone, can't sign -> firm callback (verbatim): "I understand you're on your phone right now and can't access your email to sign. That's perfectly fine. What's the soonest you'll be at a computer where we can jump back on for five minutes and get this finalized? Would this afternoon at 3 PM work, or would tomorrow morning at 9 AM be better?"
- In person: offer in a folder, walk the property with intent, don't leave without the signature; in-person lifts contract rate.
- "Let me think about it" / "talk to my spouse" = objection, not a no -> Objection Handling.

**Roles:** Cold caller = discovery (confirm DM, first-pass MCTP, book next call). Lead manager = first contact through offer (process call, cadence, roadblocks, revivals). Closer = money + commitment (present, negotiate, sign live). Each pipeline status has one owner. Solo: wear all three hats but run each stage as its own call; hire the lead manager in Phase 3 (The Manager).
- Comp the property before the money conversation, never during. Lead Manager Coach and Closer Coach skills grade calls against this playbook.

## 3. The Objection Handling Bank: 99 Objections, One Pattern (objection-handling.txt)

- 99 objections from 5 days of live challenge calls, logged with: who asked, the reasoning, one verbatim line, the lever.
- Only 2 of 99 were about price. People object with execution anxiety (data sourcing, tools, hiring) -> answer with proof, not discounts.
- **Rebuttal pattern: three beats, reasoning first, the verbatim line lands LAST** (it closes the beat, never opens it; opening with a memorized line sounds like a telemarketer). Beat three works only when you know the lever (trust, proof, pattern interrupt, moat); pull it on purpose.
- The 99 by theme: Tooling/AI setup 32; Data quality/sourcing 18; Team/hiring 15; Other deal mechanics 12; Skip tracing 8; Market selection 5; Scaling 3; Competition 3; Price 2; Lock-in/flexibility 1. Tooling + data = 50 of 99.

**Seven levers:**
1. Volunteer the cheaper path (downgrade plans, $100 Claude tier, skip the $3K-per-hire agency). Most consistent trust move.
2. Quantify everything ($10M closed volume, 93% over a 70 AI score, $20-50/mo tools, $100K closed before you hire).
3. Call out the overthinking ("You're overthinking it": curative title, heir paperwork, integration rabbit holes).
4. Sell the difficulty (hard-to-source data is the moat: no-list county scraping, deep prospecting).
5. Position discipline as protection (sequential click-to-call lowers spam flags and lawsuit risk; cold AI dialing and power dialing raise both).
6. Never change what is working (golden rule; a 6-day cadence producing contracts is not touched).
7. Route edge cases to the safety net ("That is a good support ticket": support, community, Deal Room).

**Top 10 (answer + verbatim line + lever):**
1. "Signed up for AI plan, small, starting first-to-market, need it?" -> No. Downgrade to Business, or Expert for obituary data; jump back to AI when you need more data. Line: "Yeah, you can just downgrade... and then when they need more data, they'll jump back in." Lever: cheaper path.
2. "Leaving meat on the bone excluding lower AI scores?" -> Some, but bandwidth. ~93% of $10M closed in 6 months came from records over a 70 AI score. 70-100 band ~5% of property volume. Start there; expand to 60-70 for cheap volume. Line: "The criteria of the list... over 70 AI score was, I think, 93% of the transaction volume." Lever: hard number.
3. "Trestle scored wrong numbers 100 and correct low; reliable?" -> Trestle scores ACTIVITY, not correctness. Wrong numbers always exist from skip tracing; cutting dead lines roughly halves dials to reach the same correct people. Line: "Massive, massive difference in getting a correct number versus an active number... still a massive net win on productivity." Lever: reframe the metric.
4. "Factor in competition when selecting a market?" -> Only buyer activity. Heavy competition = strong market. First-to-market data = less competition, cheaper cost per deal; then blanket-spend to outscale once producing. Line: "You want there to be a lot of competition, because if you do things like this on the challenge, you are going to be better than them." Lever: flip fear into buy signal.
5. "Three decent counties, expand for more FTM data?" -> Nail one niche in one county A to Z first (uncooperative clerks, VAs, SifStack make FTM hard to set up); then new counties flow into the same conveyor. Line: "Start with one first-to-market niche in one county, because once you've gotten that A to Z, it's literally the same thing to add in 5." Lever: kill overwhelm.
6. "How many deals/month before hiring?" -> Close $100K yourself first (~10 wholesale deals). Hold 3-6 months personal savings + 3-6 months business runway; assume 90 days for a hire to ramp. Offload admin sooner; revenue roles wait. Line: "If you haven't closed 100,000 by yourself in real estate, don't hire anybody yet. As a baseline." Lever: hard threshold.
7. "Bulk dialing: hire people or agency?" -> In-house. Agency ~50% more (~$14/hr for a $7/hr caller), lower quality, holds your biggest pipeline. If you must, hire one for 6 months, learn the process, bring it home. Line: "You're essentially saying, I'm willing to give 50% of the weight of my success into the hands of another person, and pay double the price." Lever: price convenience in margin and control.
8. "Curative title/heir deals without attorneys?" -> Do it; post curative-feeling deals in the community; a good title company handles most; title attorney is backstop. Line: "You're overthinking it." Lever: pattern interrupt.
9. "AI cold calling/texting?" -> Never cold-prospect with AI (robocalling always illegal; AI conversation more egregious). AI belongs on inbound leads and ghosted/cold-lead nurture. Click-to-call is the legal protection. Line: "Do not use AI to prospect coldly. If you do that, you're probably gonna get sued." Lever: saying no for their protection builds trust.
10. "How many extra subscriptions?" -> Outside Claude and DataSift, extras (Tracerify skip tracing, Trestle phone scoring) are pay-per-use APIs. Tracerify = 2 cents/record. Total $20-50/mo. Line: "I'm not spending more than 20 to 50 bucks a month on those extra tools." Lever: one real personal number.

**When to stop pushing:**
- 75-100 correct-number not-interesteds -> ~1 deal inside 12 months.
- 4 full attempts per record, then rest; no freelancing past it.
- Missed hot transfer: closer works it ~5 days, then back to lead management.
- Drip campaigns are for 2 audiences only: not-interesteds and ghosted leads.
- A no is a disposition: mark Not Interested, drip takes over. Arguing past a real no burns the record and the caller's next 10 minutes.
- Ghosting = an objection you never uncovered, mostly fear: "Anytime you get ghosted, it is because you did not uncover an objection, and it is due to fear, most of the time." Hence walk the contract live section by section, never send it to be read alone.
- Rami re-runs ghosted leads from a different phone number under a different name; keeps them on until signed live over DocuSign or the real objection surfaces.

## 4. Making the Offer (making-the-offer.txt)

- Offer call's one job: a signed purchase agreement, live on the call or at the kitchen table. Not a promise to review later.
- NOT math hour: no formula on this call. Numbers built beforehand from comps (Comping Workflow) and repair estimate (Rehab Estimator).
- Handoff open (verbatim): "Hi [seller name], this is [your name] with DataSift. I've been reviewing the notes from your conversation with [previous team member] and I understand you're looking to sell your property at [address] because you're tired of being a landlord and dealing with those tenants. You also mentioned you'd ideally like to close by the end of next month. Is that all still correct?" Restate motivation + timeline, get one confirmation.

**Multi-option descending offer (3 options; cash first, then two that pay more and take longer):**
- Setup line: "We have a few ways we can help. I want to show you all of them."
1. Cash ($X, 21 days): "Our all-cash offer is [$X]. This is for speed and convenience. We buy it as-is, pay all closing costs, and can close in 21 days. No repairs, no fees."
2. Novation ($Y, 60-90 days): "If you have a bit more time, we can get you more money. We can offer you [$Y]. We find a buyer, manage the process, and you get a higher price in about 60-90 days."
3. Listing ($Z, longest): "Finally, if you want top market dollar and don't mind the time and effort, we can connect you with our top agent to list it on the market. You could likely get around [$Z]."
- Close: "Which of these options sounds best for your situation?" then silence.
- More money always costs more time. Higher novation/listing numbers anchor the cash offer to look attractive. All three are real offers; present all three every time.

**Presenting price without flinching:** script the minute before the number: recap pain -> worst-case timeline -> options.
- Recap (verbatim example): "So about the property. You mentioned that you have these tenants, and they haven't been paying rent here for the last three months or so. And ultimately, it's made you go into foreclosure because you haven't been able to pay the mortgage as a result of that. And it's affecting your credit."
- Worst-case timeline: same 21-day script as Ultimate Call Playbook.
- Never sound desperate/begging.
- Do: restate motivation before any number; name worst-case close date then beat it; present all 3; ask which fits, go silent. Don't: apologize for or rush past the number; promise 7 and deliver 21; present one number and defend it; keep talking after the ask.

**Exact number, never a range.** One exact figure per option. Use lender language: the number the property "qualifies for" (output of a process, not your opinion).
- Verbatim: "As you can see, Mr. and Mrs. Seller, we have a very thorough process where we ask very detailed questions about the property condition. So we can give you the most accurate number that your property would qualify for."
- Seller price far above cash number: don't split the difference, pivot to terms (verbatim): "I understand you're looking for [seller's price] for the property, and honestly, that's a bit higher than we can pay with a straight cash offer right now. However, if we could get closer to that number, would you be open to a different way of structuring the sale? I give you the price you want, you give me the terms I need."

**No open offers:** emailed offer gets shopped. Same e-signature close + 6 parts (price/terms, closing date, as-is, closing costs, contingencies, EMD) + firm callback script as Stage 4 (callback here says "jump back on the phone for five minutes"; "this afternoon at 3 PM ... or tomorrow morning at 9 AM"). Never "I'll email it over."
- In person: offer printed in a blue folder, take photos during walkthrough, review agreement at kitchen table, don't leave without signature.

**When the number lands (4 outcomes):**
- "Yes." -> e-signature close now, review 6 parts, sign live.
- "Let me think about it." -> name the no: "A lot of times when people say, let me think about it, it's a polite way of saying no." Then box it: is it me, the process, the price, or other options?
- "Can you do more?" -> "Is there a price you have in mind that if I can get approved for you, you would award us the opportunity?" Get their number before moving yours.
- "Let me talk to my wife." -> before they hang up, find exactly what they need to run past spouse/attorney/CPA: process, price, other options?
- Inspection changes the number: CALL, never email. State what inspection found, acknowledge their price ("I know you're wanting 150K"), name the new figure, pause and listen.

## 5. Closing and Commitment (closing-commitment.txt)

**[NOTE] This page is about closing a prospect on a DataSift subscription (sales of the platform), not closing a seller.**
- A close = four small yeses in order, not one big ask. 30-minute team-led conversation (rep steers), not self-serve checkout. A small yes every 3-5 minutes. 3 yeses before price talk.
- Gates: Belief -> Capability -> Capacity -> Price. Never jump to price; price resistance = an earlier gate never closed: back up, re-close, re-ask.
  - Gate 1 Belief ("Yes"): "Do you see how this system produces deals?" Carried by operator proof, never a feature list: 305 documented success stories; $800K/yr solo operator on ~$1,500/mo marketing; $623K first-year annualized run rate from one first-to-market niche.
  - Gate 2 Capability ("I Could"): "Can you see yourself running this in your market?" Map their phase and constraint to the exact engines they'd run; specificity.
  - Gate 3 Capacity ("I Can"): "Is there any reason you couldn't start this week?" Risk reversal: 90-Day Free-Platform Guarantee makes waiting the only risky move.
  - Gate 4 Price ("No-Brainer"): recommend the phase-matched tier with conviction; one number, plainly, no inflated value stack. Turns into a negotiation -> a prior gate is open.

**Tier by phase:**
| Phase | Constraint | Dream outcome | Tier | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Operator | Capital scarcity (trades time for money) | Close first deal, prove system | Business $299/mo ($2,870/yr) | 5 users, 25K monthly records, unlimited sequences. Fallback: Professional $149/mo ($1,490/yr) |
| 2 Delegator | Time scarcity (trades money for time) | Deal machine that runs without you | Expert $499/mo ($4,990/yr) | 15 users, 50K monthly records; unlimited skip tracing + SiftMap Pro. Fallback: Business $299 |
| 3 Manager | Process and chaos (trades overhead for predictability) | Predictable revenue, sellable business | AI $1,250/mo ($12,500/yr) | 30 users, 100K monthly records; AI scoring, investor + realtor. Upshift even if prospect self-describes smaller |
| 4 Owner | Margin and access (trades complexity for margin) | Dominate a niche with $50K+ spreads | Expert $499/mo ($4,990/yr) | 15 users, 50K; fallback Business $299 |
- Do: downshift for budget/stage (lower rung = a start, not a lost sale); upshift when operation is bigger than self-description; keep alt-tier logic in conversation; recommend a real fit. Don't: push a tier they can't operate/afford; strand them too small; put the downshift ladder in a deck; treat matched tier as a hard rule.
- Back-end lever is tier mix, not whether they subscribe.

**First 24 hours, three locks: Signed, Stated, Scheduled.**
1. Signed: subscription starts at matched tier on the call.
2. Stated: 90-Day Free-Platform Guarantee conditions in plain words: attend the 5-day cohort, stay active in the Deal Room, put the system to work.
3. Scheduled: cohort seat + Deal Room on the calendar before the call ends (guarantee is conditional on showing up).
- Pricing honesty: no inflated value stacks, no midnight cart, no founder's-pricing gimmick. Only honest scarcity: fulfillment-capacity price increases, used truthfully.

**Stalls -> gate to re-close (Hormozi: every stall is blame):**
- "I need to think about it" = blames time -> Gate 3: ask what specifically stands between them and starting this week, resolve on call.
- "I can't afford it" = blames money -> Gate 1: re-anchor operator proof; tier reads against deals produced.
- "I need to talk to my spouse" = blames other people -> Gate 3: surface hidden DM early, clear path for both.
- "It's not the right time" = blames circumstances -> Gate 3: no perfect quarter; return to the guarantee.
- "I'm not sure I can do it" = blames themselves -> Gate 2: re-map phase/constraint to engines.
- "I tried something like this before" = blames a bad experience -> Gate 1: separate past attempt with documented proof, re-ask gate 1 question.
- A stalled close goes into the follow-up cadence (lead management drips/reassignment), never a dead notes file.

## 6. The Call Coaching Loop (call-coaching-loop.txt)

- Replace coaching-from-memory with evidence: every real conversation pulled, transcribed, graded against a rubric calibrated on ~300 real calls. Three coach skills (Cold Caller, Lead Manager, Closer).
- Cost: recordings already in SmrtPhone; Gemini Flash transcribes with tonality notes at ~$0.002/audio minute (~12 cents per talk hour).
- Grade from the transcript; keep the recording one click away; play the call when a score surprises you (report points to the minute).

**Loop (weekly): Record -> Transcribe -> Grade -> Scorecard -> Decide.**
1. Record (SmrtPhone records every outbound call; skill pulls call log + recordings). Default pull: calls >=60s, last 7 days. Closer calls: widen to 30 days. Calls triaged into cold call / lead management / closing before grading.
2. Transcribe (Gemini Flash; pace, tone, talk balance; delivery summary cited as evidence).
3. Grade (Claude; each dimension 0/3/5 anchors, out of 5). Verify pass checks verbatim quotes, evidence per line, math that foots. Short calls (seller declined inside ~30 s) get short-call report, no score. Auto-fails: ignored opt-outs, misrepresentation, compliance breaches.
4. Scorecard: per-call reports (quote exact lines) -> per-caller scorecard -> one styled Excel workbook tracking each caller by dimension run over run; delivered to Slack on a schedule.
5. Decide (manager only): read the trend, not one score; climbing = coach; flat on the same coached dimension = replace conversation. One dimension per caller per week.

**Rubrics:**
- Cold Caller Coach (frame: "correct-number-gatherers, not deal-closers"): Opener + DM confirmation 15%; Four Pillars discovery 30%; Objection handling + list sensitivity 20%; Tonality + call control 15%; Close + next step 20%.
- Lead Manager Coach: Opening + continuity 10%; Four Pillars qualification 30%; Roadblocks + logistics (title, occupancy, who signs) 20%; Rapport, tonality, call control 20%; Next action + handoff (dated next step) 20%. Call quality only; CRM hygiene not scored.
- Closer Coach: Handoff open + discovery deepening 15%; Money conversation + offer presentation 25% (number with a reason, not an apology); Objection handling + negotiation 20% (concessions traded not given); Commitment locking + next steps 25%; Tonality + human delivery 10%; ABL discipline + offer gating 5% (no offer before qualified). Extras per call: negotiation timeline (every number, who moved, why) and "the moment it was won or lost". 30-day pull window; run post-mortem on a lost deal or pre-game before a big offer call.

**Scorecard as meeting agenda:**
1. Read by dimension, not average (a 4.0 caller with a 2 on openers has one target).
2. One fix per caller per week.
3. Huddle: daily 30-min while scores move, or weekly best-call/worst-call review; play both recordings (reports give timestamps).
4. Log the coached dimension; next scorecard answers "did that number move".
- Play the call, don't describe it.

**Coach or replace:**
- Effort floors: 150 dials/day per caller (target 200); 15-min warm-up (script reps, tonality drills) before first live dial; rotate outbound numbers ~every 25 dials; free Call Reputation Monitor skill reads your answer rates for spam flags.
- Coach when: scores climb even slowly; gap in 1-2 dimensions; effort floors met; hire inside the 90-day ramp.
- Replace when: same coached dimension flatlines scorecard after scorecard; effort floors keep being missed (effort is a choice, skill is trainable); auto-fails appear.
- Never move a struggling hire across roles (a caller who can't open doesn't become a lead manager).
- You can only coach a job you've done: close ~$100K (~10 wholesale deals) yourself before hiring.
- Caller costs $750-1K/month; replacement restarts the 90-day ramp. Trend up -> coaching cheaper; flat -> waiting is expensive.

## 7. Deep Prospecting (deep-prospecting.txt)

- The 22-38% of any list sequential marketing can't reach (dead owners, wrong addresses, heirs with no probate). Example: 330 tax sale records, skip trace, 27-touch sequence -> reach ~2/3.
- Two motions: (1) ESCALATION TAIL for unreachable records; (2) PAIRED LEAD MOTION: on deceased-owner lists (native SiftMap obituary, pre-probate, estate) deep-prospect from the FIRST touch.
- Obituary = most first-to-market source; native premium distressor in SiftMap, nearly uncontested. Access: $499/mo Expert (SiftMap Pro + unlimited skip trace) or SiftMap Pro add-on (~$297/mo) on $149/mo Professional; no AI plan needed. Anchors: ~12% contact rate, ~7% lead rate off a skip trace alone; Pre-Probate/Deceased ~14.8 doors per deal.
- Channel rule: call, voicemail, direct mail to the family, then deep-prospect heir/executor. Never cold text a deceased/obituary record; never open by mentioning the death. Opener: "do you have any plans for the property?"
- Stats: $1.50-4 cost per touch; 22-38% deceased owners on lists; 0 competing investors; ~90% wrong numbers from skip traces.
- Restricted marketing skip traces return ~10 possible numbers/record, 1 correct -> ~90% wrong is normal; a "failed" skip trace means verify which number is real.

**Four levels:**
1. Enhanced Skip Tracing: DataSift built-in skip trace first, then Skip Genie second pass; with a real estate license, Forewarn has the best data. $0.15/record - $97/mo. Bulk.
2. Ownership Verification: conflicting names/addresses/history -> pull deed chain from county recorder; middle initials, transaction types, installment agreements show whether recorded owner is the actual DM. County Recorder, Google. Free-$25. 15-30 min/record.
3. Deceased Owner / Heir Research: owner dead, no probate -> genealogy, obituary, family tree. "Go back to go forward" (research older records and deceased relatives to find paths to living heirs). FindAGrave, Ancestry. $25-50/mo. 1-3 hrs/record.
4. Curative Title Work: multiple generations, partial ownership disputes, clouded title needing court action; often needs title attorney. Title Attorney, PACER. $500-2,000+. Days to weeks. Massive deals, nobody else has patience.
- Most investors never pass Level 1; the money is in Levels 2 and 3.

**Three phases: Verify -> Research -> Locate.** If owner is alive, skip Phase 2 (Research) entirely.
- 3-Site Skip Trace Waterfall (all free): run the DM's name through TruePeopleSearch -> FastPeopleSearch -> CyberBackgroundChecks. Same phone on 2+ sites = high-confidence match.

**Tools by phase (start free, upgrade when volume demands):**
- Verify: County Deed Records (Free-$25; transfer dates, middle initials, "estate of" flags, installment agreements); Google Dorking (free; e.g. `site:findagrave.com` + owner name for quick death verification); BeenVerified ($27-37/mo; primary DP skip tool; frame search as "title work" for best results; $1 trial).
- Research: FindAGrave.com (free; death records, cemetery, family connections; first stop for death verification); Ancestry.com ($24.99-49.99/mo; U.S. Discovery plan $24.99/mo sufficient for heir research); Newspapers.com (included with Ancestry; historical obituaries, public notices).
- Locate: TruePeopleSearch (free; first; check "Also Known As" for maiden names/aliases; cross-ref relatives); FastPeopleSearch (free; second); CyberBackgroundChecks (free; third; check "Relatives/Associates").

**Heir Verification Loop (6 steps):**
1. Confirm death: FindAGrave + Google owner name + "obituary"; death date must match deed inactivity timeline.
2. Read the obituary: extract every name (spouse, children, grandchildren, siblings); distinguish "survived by" vs "preceded in death by".
3. Build family tree (Ancestry): "Sr." naming proves at least one child exists; cross-ref maiden names from marriage records.
4. Mark living vs deceased: FindAGrave, Social Security Death Index, Facebook; dead heirs create more branches.
5. Identify DM: surviving spouse (right of survivorship) or heir most likely to control; prioritize simplest chain of title.
6. Run 3-site waterfall; phone on 2+ sites = high confidence; update CRM with heir name and new mailing address.
- Do: FindAGrave before paying for Ancestry; document every heir in CRM with status (alive/deceased/unknown); use "Owner Swap" in DataSift to update to heir's name/address; process returned mail records first (highest ROI).
- Don't: skip the loop and cold-call every skip-trace number; assume one skip provider gives everything; ignore "estate of" designations; deep-prospect records that still have working phones (sequential marketing first).

## 8. Buyer Prospecting (buyer-prospecting.txt)

- Buyers list = insurance; built early turns a 60-day prayer into a 2-day assignment. Have a VA do the whole county.
- Do: build before you have a deal; research DMs behind LLCs; track patterns (what, where, how much); prospect buyers with niche sequential. Don't: wait for a contract; blast every "cash buyer" list; skip trace the LLC instead of the person; rely on one Facebook post.

**Investor transaction types:**
- Wholesale (<72 hours hold): never renovates; needs deepest discount (adds margin layer). SiftMap tell: multiple purchases in short timeframes, rarely appears as seller. Fastest close, lowest price.
- Wholetail (<90 days): paint/carpet/clean, lists on MLS. Tell: buy/sell cycle <90 days, sale price 10-25% above purchase. Pays more than wholesale, less negotiation.
- Flip (90 days - 18 months): full renovation, sells retail. Tell: buy/sell spread 90d-18mo, significant price increase. Highest price tolerance; best for heavy rehab.
- Buy-and-Hold (18+ months): cash-flow evaluation (rental market > ARV). Buys cash-flowing, multifamily, lower-price SFRs. Tell: 5+ purchases, no matching sales, entity names (LLCs, trusts). Consistent repeat buyers.

**SiftMap buyer presets (both 18-month lookback):**
- Recent Active Investors (Pro): Transaction Type = Investor; Min Properties Purchased 3+; lookback 18 months; requires SiftMap Pro ($297/mo, included in Expert $499+).
- Quick Resale / Flippers (All plans): Transaction Type = Quick Resale; resale window <12 months; lookback 18 months.
- "I would rather have 20 people that I know buy in my area consistently than 200 random people on a cash buyers list."

**3-Signal Buyer Read:** Volume (total purchased), Velocity (how fast: 10 in 6 months != 10 in 6 years), Recency (last purchase; last month = ready, 3 years ago = maybe stopped).
- Example: SMP Holdings LLC bought 3012 Lay Ave, Knoxville for $63,000 cash; $442,000 portfolio, 100% equity; two Knox properties, both cash.

**Buyer Prospector Skill (Claude Pro $20+/mo):** tells you who is behind the entity (e.g. not "Solid Ground Sales LLC" but Julie Winter).
1. Download .skill (analysis script + nationwide buyer DB 84,000+ records across 1,471 counties + research guides).
2. Load into a new Claude project.
3. Specify county/state (Knox TN: 138 active buyers).
4. Entity categorization: LLC, Trust, Corporation, Estate, Individual (Knox: 112 LLCs, 11 trusts, 9 corps, 5 individuals).
5. DM research: Secretary of State databases, OpenCorporates, secondary sources -> registered agent/managing member/principal (Knox: 84% identification rate).
6. Excel, 3 tabs: Found; Not Found (manual research); All Records (entity categorization + priority ranking).
7. Review and clean: remove iBuyers (Opendoor, Offerpad) and institutional buyers; prioritize by volume and recency. Top 20 local buyers > 500 random LLCs.

**Prospecting the list:** Filter Saved (buyer contacts in CRM) -> Push to Prospect -> Niche Sequential (call, text, mail cadence) -> Buyer Relationship. Goal is learning criteria, not pitching: "What are you looking for in Knox County right now?"

**Verified buyers list (Phonebook = clean rolodex, not a dump):**
1. Prospect first via niche sequential: cold call first, then text, then mail. Text is never the first touch.
2. Add only after speaking and they confirm they want deals: Phonebook -> "Add New Contact" -> name + phone (required) -> Contact Status "Buyer" -> tag by transaction type (wholesale, flip, buy-and-hold).
3. Existing DataSift record: open Owner Details -> toggle "Mark as Contact" (converts without duplicating).

- Don't dump every skill-output number into Phonebook, add unverified contacts, skip prospecting to "I have a deal", or treat Phonebook as a cold call list.

**Buyer Finder AI (coming, expected 3-6 months):** 0-100 buyer score; purchase price recommendations; buyer-type filtering; portfolio view (every purchase, avg hold time, preferred neighborhoods). Today's manual workflow (SiftMap presets -> export entity list -> Buyer Prospector skill -> DM research -> upload contacts -> niche sequential) takes 2-4 hours setup per county.

## 9. AI-Powered Comping Workflow (comping-workflow.txt)

- Traditional comping 45-90 min/property. Claude comping skill (9-step; ~25 iterations; peer-reviewed against closed deals) takes an address, pulls public records, finds comps, two-buckets them, adjusts, checks market, outputs confidence-banded ARV range.
- Output: 7-tab Excel: Executive Summary, Subject Property, Comparable Sales, Adjustments Detail, Market Analysis, ARV Calculation, Sources and Notes.
- Needs: Claude Pro ($20/mo; no Max needed), an address, optionally your closed-deal data. Upload .skill to Claude Co-Work or Claude Projects (download from Google Drive).
- Intentionally conservative: ARV should be the floor, not the ceiling ("A high ARV that does not hold up kills deals").

**Two-Bucket Method:**
- Bucket A (Unrenovated): dated, original, deferred maintenance; similar age/size/layout; similar size/plan, average or below condition; sold within 90 days (6 months max); arm's-length only -> Median PPSF_A.
- Bucket B (Renovated): flips / clearly modernized, verified via photos, remarks, permits; quality-adjust ultra-lux outliers -> Median PPSF_B.
- Market Premium (%) = (PPSF_B - PPSF_A) / PPSF_A x 100%. Market-derived, NOT a rehab cost. Typical 10-30%; below 5% or above 30% re-examine comp selection. At <=5% flipping doesn't pay; at 35%+ recheck buckets. [NOTE: page says both ">30% re-examine" and "35%+ recheck".]

**Disclosure vs non-disclosure (auto-detected by address):**
- Disclosure (38 states): real sold prices from MLS, county records, Zillow, Redfin; confidence band +/-2-5%.
- Non-disclosure (12 states): TX, UT, WY, NM, ID, MT, ND, AK, KS, MS, LA, MO. Triangulate estimated sold price (ESP), then Two-Bucket; band +/-5-7%.
  - Method A, last list price + DOM (last list before "Pending"): <7 DOM = list or 101%; 7-30 DOM = 97-100%; >30 DOM = 90-95%.
  - Method B, deed of trust: Conventional Loan/0.80; FHA Loan/0.965; VA Loan/1.00.
  - Method C, tax value ratio: assessed-to-list ratios on actives, apply multiplier; confirmation only, never primary.

## 10. Rehab Estimator Workflow (rehab-estimator-workflow.txt)

- Core idea: rehab estimating is about knowing what NOT to include. Claude Rehab Estimator skill: room-by-room scopes, regional material pricing, 9-tab Excel. Inputs: inspection photos + comp report. Claude Pro $20/mo; upload skill and activate in Project settings. Dialed in over 4 iterations.

- 60-65% rule: per-SF cost x 0.60-0.65 ~= materials-only cost. Materials >65% of total = labor underpriced; <50% = maybe over-scoping materials. Standard flip at $25-35/SF -> materials $15-23/SF.
- >$40/SF on a standard cosmetic flip = almost certainly over-scoping.

**7-step process:**
1. Gather intel: inspection report, photos, CRM notes from homeowner conversation, comp report. Inspection reports give best results; photos alone a distant second. Investor Bootz = investor-focused inspections.
2. Photo analysis: room-by-room condition assessment.
3. Read calibration data (your SOWs), else national benchmarks by region.
4. Build scope room-by-room: paint, flooring, outlets/switches ($100/room), light fixtures, doors. Second pass for transitional spaces (hallways, mudroom, back porch, laundry, foyer) adds $3,000-4,000 typical.
5. Localize pricing: materials from Amazon, Lowe's, Home Depot by region; labor multiplier 0.7x (low-cost rural) to 1.5x (high-cost metro).
6. Cross-reference comps: renovation premium (B minus A PPSF) sets the finish-level ceiling. Thin gap = market doesn't reward heavy reno; wide gap = higher tier justified.
7. Deliverables, 9 tabs: Summary, Full Rehab Estimate, Wholetail Estimate, Condition Assessment, Material Spec, Deal Analyzer, Budget Tracker, Project Checklist, Comp Report reference.

**Wholetail vs full rehab (profit-per-month test; comp report picks the exit, skill builds both):**
- Full rehab: $35-60/SF (Tier 3); 8-16 wks rehab + 1-2 mo marketing + 1 mo closing = 4-6 mo hold; retail buyer via MLS at full ARV, show-ready. **Market adjustments (automatic; you validate):**
